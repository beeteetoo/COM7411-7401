# 02 — Research philosophy and paradigms

**Goal:** justify a philosophical position for a particular proposed study and respond fairly to an alternative. This supports LO1 and the Critical Literature Review.

Your **ontological** position concerns what you assume the phenomenon is like. Your **epistemological** position concerns what would count as knowledge about it. **Axiology** asks how values affect the work. These are design decisions to explain, not badges attached permanently to computer science, data science or cybersecurity. In computing, a *domain ontology* is a formal description of entities and relationships in a system; it is a different use of the word from philosophical ontology.

**Worked example.** Suppose your question concerns whether a security dashboard helps analysts make better incident decisions. One study could measure time and classification accuracy under controlled conditions. Another could investigate how analysts interpret the dashboard in their work setting. Neither approach is automatically superior. The question, desired evidence and limits of each approach determine the choice. A mixed study might use both, but must say how the strands inform one conclusion.

**Counter-position test.** State the strongest reasonable alternative to your preferred position. Explain what that alternative could reveal, what it may miss for *your* question, and why your selected approach still fits. Avoid claims such as “all cybersecurity research is realist” or “all interviews are interpretivist.”

**Glossary:** *ontology* — assumptions about the nature of what is studied; *epistemology* — assumptions about how it can be known; *paradigm* — a linked set of assumptions that guides inquiry; *counter-position* — a credible alternative to evaluate.

**Your task:** write four sentences: your question, your provisional philosophical position, the evidence it allows you to use, and one serious counter-position. The aim is justification, not naming every philosophy.

**Further reading:** Mbanaso, Abrahams and Okafor (2023), Chapter 6, through a licensed library route if available.
