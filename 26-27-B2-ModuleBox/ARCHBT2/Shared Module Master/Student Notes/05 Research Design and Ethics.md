# 05 — Research design and ethics

**Goal:** justify a coherent, feasible and ethical route from question to evidence. This supports LO2 and the Methodology section.

A design is the plan that connects your question, what would count as an answer, sources of data, collection procedure and analysis. Quantitative, qualitative, mixed-method and design-and-creation approaches may all be suitable in computing. If you propose a prototype, specify what the evaluation will teach us. If you mix methods, say *where* the strands meet: for example, interview findings could explain an unexpected pattern in usage data.

**Worked example.** To study why staff ignore a security warning, a proposal might combine anonymised click-log counts with interviews about decision context. The logs show frequency; interviews may explain circumstances. The design must specify who can access the logs, how participants are recruited, what consent covers, and how identities are protected. The two types of evidence need an explicit integration step.

Ethics is part of method choice. Consider consent, potential harm, power imbalance, privacy, data security, retention, researcher safety and transparent reporting. A low-risk secondary dataset still needs a legitimate access route and careful handling. Local university approval processes must be checked against current institutional guidance; examples in a textbook are not an approval decision.

**Glossary:** *research design* — the overall logic of an inquiry; *method* — a way of collecting or analysing evidence; *mixed methods* — planned use and integration of qualitative and quantitative evidence; *mitigation* — a proportionate step to reduce a specific risk.

**Your task:** draw a chain from question to evidence, method, analysis and ethical safeguard. Name one credible alternative design and explain why you did not choose it. Complete the Ethics Self-Assessment Worksheet before the design checkpoint. Do not collect real participant data for this portfolio exercise.

**Further reading:** Mbanaso, Abrahams and Okafor (2023), Chapter 6, plus the module Ethics Pack.
