# 06 — Sampling and data collection

**Goal:** specify what data a proposed study needs, where it would come from and how it could be collected safely. This supports LO2 and the Methodology section.

Start with the research question. Name the **unit of analysis** (for example, a software run, incident, document, person or organisation), the population or source, and the selection rule. A sample is justified by its fit to the question and the claims you intend to make, not only by size. Distinguish primary data that you would collect from existing data that you would reuse. A proposed survey, interview, log analysis or experiment needs an instrument or procedure capable of producing the right evidence.

**Worked example.** To compare two intrusion-alert interfaces, a proposal might use a set of simulated alerts and recruit analysts who meet stated experience criteria. It would define the comparison task, the outcome measures and how order effects would be handled. If access to professional analysts is unlikely, a secondary dataset or a narrower usability question may be more feasible. The proposal must not claim that a convenience sample represents all analysts.

For participant work, draft consent information and decide how identifiable data would be protected. For technical data, check provenance, permission, security and whether sensitive records are present. Treat an instrument as a draft to be evaluated; do not actually recruit participants as part of this portfolio.

**Glossary:** *unit of analysis* — the thing each observation describes; *sampling frame* — the set from which cases could be selected; *instrument* — a survey, interview guide, measure or protocol; *secondary data* — data originally gathered for another purpose.

**Your task:** complete a five-row table: question, unit/source, selection rule, instrument/procedure and ethical/feasibility safeguard. Write one sample survey item, interview question or evaluation measure that clearly serves your question.
