# Ethics primer for the Week 3 design checkpoint

Use this with the [Ethics Self-Assessment Worksheet](../../Ethics%20Pack%20%28Independent%20Study%29/Ethics%20Self-Assessment%20Worksheet.md). Ethics is part of choosing a method, not a paragraph added after the design is fixed.

**People and consent.** If your proposed study involves people, explain what they would be asked to do, how they could choose freely, and whether a power relationship might affect that choice. A proposal should make withdrawal, confidentiality and the limits of anonymity clear where relevant.

**Risk and fairness.** Consider physical, emotional, reputational and digital harms; who might bear them; and whether the benefits and burdens are distributed fairly. A security or AI study can expose sensitive behaviour or bias even when it uses familiar technical tools. Name a specific mitigation rather than writing “risk is low.”

**Data and access.** State what would be collected, whether it identifies anyone directly or indirectly, who could access it, how it would be stored and when it would be deleted. Existing datasets still require a legitimate access route and careful handling. Do not assume a public website or a textbook example grants permission for a new study.

**Integrity.** Plan how you would report limitations and inconvenient findings. Cite the work you draw on and follow the assessment brief's separate GAI rules for the report and recorded defence. A GAI output is not an academic source or evidence about your proposed study.

**Approval.** The portfolio asks you to *plan* an ethical study. It does not authorise data collection. Any future real study must follow the university's current approval and data-governance procedures. The ethics-clearance examples in the research methods book come from other settings and do not determine local approval.

**Your checkpoint:** identify one participant or data-related risk in your own proposal, its likely consequence, a proportionate mitigation and the design choice that mitigation changes. Bring this answer to the Week 3 session or submit it through the equivalent COM7411 route.
