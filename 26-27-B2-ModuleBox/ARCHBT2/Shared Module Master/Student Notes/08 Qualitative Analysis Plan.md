# 08 — Qualitative analysis plan

**Goal:** show how text or other qualitative material could support a defensible interpretation. This supports LO2 and LO3.

Read a short extract more than once. Mark meaningful units, give them provisional codes, compare codes across extracts and consider a candidate theme. A theme is an interpretive claim relevant to the question, not simply a frequent word. Keep a trail from quotation or observation to code and interpretation, and look for data that challenge your first reading. The method you name in your portfolio should fit both the question and the planned material.

**Worked example.** The invented `08 Sample Transcript.md` contains short comments from three fictional staff about a security warning. You might initially code “too many alerts” as **alert overload** and “I checked with a colleague” as **social verification**. A candidate theme could be that warning use depends on workflow and trust, but another analyst could reasonably refine the codes. Explain the steps and the evidence for your interpretation.

For a real proposed study, say how you would select material, protect people, store recordings or text, and check rigour. Possible checks include keeping an audit trail, searching for disconfirming cases and making your own role in interpretation explicit. Do not treat a textbook coding example as a substitute for analysing your actual proposed data type.

**Glossary:** *code* — a concise label assigned to a meaningful segment; *theme* — an interpretive pattern relevant to the question; *audit trail* — a record of decisions from raw material to interpretation; *reflexivity* — attention to how the researcher's position shapes the analysis.

**Your task:** code four lines of the sample transcript, propose one theme, quote two short phrases that support it, and note one rival reading. Then state how the method would answer your own research question.

**Further reading:** Mbanaso, Abrahams and Okafor (2023), Chapters 6–7, through a licensed library route if available.
