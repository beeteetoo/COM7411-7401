# 03 — From topic to research question

**Goal:** narrow a broad topic into a question that a feasible study could answer. This supports LO1 and LO2 and becomes the Background section's anchor.

Use a funnel: **field → concern → published evidence → specific gap or tension → scoped problem → research question → evidence needed**. A gap is not merely “few papers found.” It may be conflicting findings, an untested context, a weak measure or a design limitation. Check the proposed question against the time, data access and ethics constraints of a five-week proposal task.

**Worked example.** “Build an AI app for student wellbeing” names a product task and a very broad purpose. A researchable version might ask: “How accurately does a simple, locally run text classifier identify requests for academic support in an anonymised example dataset compared with a keyword baseline?” The study would specify the dataset, labels, comparison measure and limitations. An artefact may be built, but the finding is the warranted comparison.

**Question checks.** Can you name the phenomenon, group or setting? Is an important term defined? Could evidence reasonably answer the question? Would the method still be necessary if the expected answer were not what you hoped? Does the question ask for knowledge rather than only list build steps?

**Glossary:** *scope* — the boundary of the study; *gap* — a justified uncertainty in current knowledge; *baseline* — a comparison point; *operational definition* — how a concept will be recognised or measured in this study.

**Your task:** create a six-box funnel, then write one main question and two sentences describing the evidence required. Ask a peer to identify the first undefined word or hidden assumption. Revise the question once.

**Further reading:** Mbanaso, Abrahams and Okafor (2023), Chapter 4, through a licensed library route if available.
