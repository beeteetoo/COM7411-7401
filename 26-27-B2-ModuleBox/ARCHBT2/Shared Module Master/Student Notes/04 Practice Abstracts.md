# Invented abstracts for literature-appraisal practice

These are **fictional teaching abstracts**, not academic sources. Students must use real, cited sources in the assessed literature review.

## A — Controlled comparison

A research team compared two warning messages during a simulated phishing-recognition task with 48 volunteer students. One message gave a generic “unsafe site” alert; the other identified the suspicious address. The second group classified more imitation pages correctly. Participants were not randomly sampled from staff, and the study did not observe real workplace behaviour.

## B — Workplace case study

Researchers interviewed 12 staff members in one organisation about how they respond to browser warnings. Participants reported that time pressure, previous false alarms and advice from colleagues shaped their decisions. The interviews explain possible meanings and contexts but cannot estimate how often every staff member ignores warnings. The organisation selected interviewees who were available during a busy period.

## C — Interface proposal

A design paper presents a new warning interface with an explanation panel and a short action checklist. It argues that the design could reduce confusion and describes a prototype. The paper reports no user evaluation, comparison task or evidence of changed behaviour. Its contribution is a proposed design and reasoning, not a demonstrated effectiveness result.

**Task:** identify each paper's claim, method/evidence, one strength, one limitation and relevance to a proposed question about security warnings. Then write a paragraph comparing at least two. For the actual portfolio, replace all three with real sources you have read and cited.
