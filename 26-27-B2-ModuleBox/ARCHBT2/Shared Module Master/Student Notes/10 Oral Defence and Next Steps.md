# 10 — Four-minute methodological defence

**Goal:** explain and defend the link between the research question in your locked report and your chosen method. This supports LO1–LO3.

The approved assessment is a **four-minute recorded response**, with a 30-second tolerance. It is not the full-panel viva described in general research textbooks and not the old 6–8 minute presentation in earlier session guides. State your submitted research question accurately, name the methodological check assigned to you, apply that check to your own proposal and explain a defensible conclusion or refinement. The report cannot be revised after it is locked.

**A useful response shape:** question and check (about 30 seconds); the key design decision (about one minute); how the check tests that decision using your proposed evidence (about two minutes); a concise conclusion and limitation (about 30 seconds). This is a practice structure, not a script to submit. Practise explaining the reasoning in your own words. Follow the assessment brief's rule that GAI is not permitted for the defence, and include the required software/GAI declaration for your assessment.

**Worked example.** If your question concerns whether a prototype improves a specific decision, a strong defence explains what comparison and outcome measure would test that claim. “I built the prototype” does not by itself answer whether it improves the decision.

**Glossary:** *oral defence* — reasoned explanation of a research decision; *methodological alignment* — fit between question, evidence and method; *counterargument* — a serious challenge to one's preferred design.

**Your task:** use the approved four-check guide once it is issued. Practise a timed response to a *practice* check, obtain feedback on clarity and evidence, then record your response to the **assigned** check after your report has been locked. Do not substitute a general summary of your proposal.

**Further reading:** Mbanaso, Abrahams and Okafor (2023), Chapters 3 and 8, for general proposal-defence principles; the current module brief controls this assessment.
