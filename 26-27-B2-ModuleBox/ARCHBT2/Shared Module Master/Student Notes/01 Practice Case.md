# Invented practice case: a clearer browser warning

This is a **fictional teaching case**, not a published impact study and not evidence that a product works.

A university IT team notices that some staff enter credentials into imitation login pages. The team proposes a browser warning that highlights a mismatch between the expected university address and the page currently open. They can build a prototype, but do not yet know whether its wording helps staff recognise the risk.

The team considers a small, simulated task in which volunteers see a set of imitation and genuine login pages. Half would see the current warning and half the proposed one. The team would record correct classifications, false alarms and time taken, then ask participants whether the warning was understandable. Participation would be voluntary, no real credentials would be entered, and any future study would require the appropriate university approvals.

**Discussion prompts:**

1. What is the research problem, and what is only the product objective?
2. Which claim could the proposed comparison support? Which larger claim could it not support?
3. What ethical or feasibility issue would you address before running this as a real study?
4. What evidence of impact *beyond* the simulated task would be needed before claiming the warning changed organisational security outcomes?
