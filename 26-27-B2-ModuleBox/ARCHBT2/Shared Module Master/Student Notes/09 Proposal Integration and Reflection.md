# 09 — Proposal integration and reflection

**Goal:** make the report read as one justified argument and evaluate its limits. This supports all three learning outcomes.

The four assessed headings have different jobs. **Background & Context** states the problem and question. **Critical Literature Review** evaluates sources and explains the gap. **Proposed Methodology** shows how a feasible, ethical design could answer the question. **Reflection** evaluates decisions, limits and the quality standards you would use if the study were carried out. Each section should make the next necessary; repeated definitions are less useful than a clear chain of reasoning.

**Worked example.** If a literature review says prior warning studies mainly measured click-through rate, the methodology could propose adding interviews about why staff act on or ignore warnings. The reflection might then acknowledge that a small purposive interview sample would offer contextual insight but not a population estimate. It could name an audit trail and search for disconfirming cases as planned quality checks.

Peer review should ask: Is the question identical throughout? Does the literature justify the method? Could the proposed data answer the question? Are risks and mitigations specific? Does the planned analysis fit the data? Is the limitation candid and linked to the design? Use the current assessment brief for word count, headings, referencing, AI declaration and submission requirements.

**Glossary:** *coherence* — sections supporting one argument; *limitation* — a justified boundary of what the design could establish; *rigour* — disciplined, transparent reasoning and procedure; *reflection* — evaluation of one's own choices and their consequences.

**Your task:** swap a full draft with a peer. Record two strengths, two specific revisions and one question about the method–evidence link. Revise your report before its lock. Write a short reflection that names a real trade-off in your design.

**Further reading:** Mbanaso, Abrahams and Okafor (2023), Chapters 3, 5 and 8, through a licensed library route if available.
