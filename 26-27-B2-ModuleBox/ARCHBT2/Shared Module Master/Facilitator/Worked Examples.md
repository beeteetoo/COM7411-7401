# Worked examples and misconception key — staff only

Use this with the ten original student notes. These are *illustrations of sound reasoning*, not scripts students must reproduce. Ask for an individual output even when the live activity is collaborative.

| Topic | What a sound response includes | Probe or misconception to address |
|---|---|---|
| 01 Foundations | A bounded problem, why it matters, uncertainty and plausible evidence. The browser-warning example needs an outcome measure and comparison. | “I built an app” is a development output. Ask: “What would you learn by evaluating it?” |
| 02 Philosophy | A stated position linked to what evidence the question needs, plus a fair alternative. | Philosophical ontology is not a taxonomy or knowledge graph. “CS is always positivist” overgeneralises. Ask what would be missed by the chosen stance. |
| 03 Funnel | Topic → documented tension → scoped problem → answerable question → evidence. A build proposal needs an evaluative finding. | “No one has researched this” requires a credible search trail. “Improve AI” has no defined population, outcome or comparison. |
| 04 Literature | Three genuine sources compared on method, evidence and relevance, ending with a justified gap. | Source summaries in separate paragraphs are not synthesis. A design paper does not prove its artefact works. |
| 05 Design | A complete question–data–method–analysis chain, one rejected alternative and risks linked to actual data/participants. | Avoid “mixed methods gives the best of both” without saying where findings meet. Do not invent ethics approval. |
| 06 Collection | Unit of analysis, source, selection rule, instrument and safeguard match the proposed question. | A convenience sample rarely supports population claims. A survey item must measure a construct, not combine two questions. |
| 07 Quantitative | In `07 Sample Data.csv`, Current has **3/5 correct flags** and median **27 s**; Proposed has **4/5 correct flags** and median **20 s**. The exercise describes ten invented cases only. | Do not infer causation or generalise from this tiny, invented, unrandomised dataset. Median is the third sorted value in each five-row group. |
| 08 Qualitative | Plausible codes include alert overload, explanation, social verification, action ambiguity, evidence of threat and time pressure. A candidate theme links usable explanation and workflow context to responses. | A theme is not a word count. Two short quotations should support it; students should name a rival reading, such as differences in interface design. |
| 09 Integration | The same question appears throughout; literature justifies a method; the method yields analyzable evidence; reflection evaluates a real trade-off and suitable quality check. | “There were no limitations” is not credible. Report sections should connect rather than repeat textbook definitions. |
| 10 Defence | Student states locked report's question, names assigned check, applies it to the question–method chain and reaches a bounded judgement within 4 min ±30 s. | A general proposal summary does not apply a check. The book's full viva procedure does not define this assessment. Use approved check definitions. |

## Facilitator routine

Before class, read the mapped book section and the note, test the task yourself, and prepare a local example. During class, model one step and then stop explaining so students can apply it. At the exit check, collect the individual output or a concise progress statement. After class, note the two most common misconceptions and address them in the next session or Wednesday COM7401 Zoom.

The book is a disciplinary source, not a local policy manual. Chapter 5's APA and long annotated bibliography exercise, Chapter 7's ethics waiver discussion and Chapter 8's full viva are not the instructions for this portfolio. In computing examples, make clear that a design-and-creation project becomes research through an explicit question and rigorous evaluation, and that philosophical ontology is distinct from a domain ontology.
