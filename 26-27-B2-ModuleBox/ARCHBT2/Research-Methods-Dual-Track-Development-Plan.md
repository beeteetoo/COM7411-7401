## 3. Pedagogy and material architecture

### Shared content pack for each topic

Maintain ten master topic packs, one for each current AM/PM theme. Each pack contains an edited slide deck; an accessible, concise note sheet and glossary; one required preparation item plus optional depth; a computing/data/cyber worked example; a common student task brief with a defined individual output; a facilitator answer/misconception key; and links to the relevant portfolio criterion and learning outcome. Keep staff answers outside student folders. Use one version-controlled master and link it from both Moodle shells; use code-specific covers, timetables and activity guides rather than duplicate academic content.

The book's printed pages anchor the packs: Week 1 Chapters 1–3 (pp. 1–36) and philosophy in Chapter 6 (pp. 81–96); Week 2 Chapters 4–5 (pp. 39–79); Week 3 Chapter 6 design (pp. 97–112) and Chapter 7 collection (pp. 115–129); Week 4 Chapter 7 analysis (pp. 130–137) with Chapter 6 qualitative analysis (pp. 109–111); Week 5 Chapters 3, 5 and 8 (pp. 139–153). Use separate, more complete material for ethics, mixed-method application, detailed quantitative/qualitative analysis, reflection, and the current assessment/AI rules. Teach philosophical ontology separately from a computing domain ontology; present design-and-creation as possible research when an artefact is rigorously evaluated. Adapt the book's APA and long-proposal examples to the assessment's Harvard and 2,500-word format, and its full viva to the four-minute defence. Do not teach its institution-specific ethics waiver language as University of Bolton policy.

### COM7401: structured campus teaching

Use the validated flipped model with a consistent **prepare → short explanation → worked example → guided practice → independent application → feedback/exit check** routine. Give researchers who are newer to teaching a timed script for each block, precise questions, likely misconceptions, board/slide cues, group-size and role instructions, a model answer, and a short fallback when a class runs late. One substantial task per session is the default. Build in low-stakes writing before discussion, peer comparison before whole-class speaking, explicit vocabulary and accessible print/digital versions. This routine supports confidence and participation without requiring staff to improvise activities.

Wednesday Zoom is additional for COM7401. Its five workshops should: (1) consolidate foundations and bridge to a research question; (2) improve source search and appraisal while preparing the ethics worksheet; (3) apply ethics cases to Monday's design/collection decisions; (4) demonstrate analysis and referencing tools and check the report plan; (5) rehearse the four methodological checks and resolve defence logistics. No essential outcome should depend solely on this workshop, because both tracks must reach the same goal. A short exit response should feed the next campus activity; it is formative, with a catch-up route.

### COM7411: designed live online learning at three scales

Use the same ten content packs in Monday/Saturday order. A predictable online cycle is **prepare asynchronously → retrieve and vote → short tutor model → investigate together → create a shared artifact → apply individually → receive feedback → consolidate on the board**. Combine teaching, social and cognitive presence; offer choice of voice, chat or written contribution; caption/record mini-inputs and provide accessible notes. Breakouts, polls and collaborative documents are delivery options rather than essential dependencies.


| Live cohort size                  | Default interaction                                                                                                            | Facilitator load and evidence                                                                                                          |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1–5, including a cohort of three | Tutor-led roundtable or rotating roles; all students speak or write on the same case. No artificial breakout room.             | Tutor can probe each learner; every learner submits the individual output.                                                             |
| 6–24                             | Pairs or groups of 3–4 in short breakout cycles with a common task board and named roles.                                     | Visit groups selectively; use one shared response per group plus each learner's output.                                                |
| 25–50                            | Poll and re-poll, then rooms of 4–5 with a recorder, challenger and reporter; sample a few reports for whole-class synthesis. | Use visible task instructions and a shared board so one tutor need not enter every room. Use individual exit responses to reveal gaps. |

For example, the ontology task has one target across all formats: each student justifies a position for their own research question and fairly addresses one alternative. With three online learners, rotate realist, constructivist and challenger roles around one case, then write individual positions. With roughly 50, use a pre-poll, room-based claim/evidence/counterargument boards, a sample of room reports and a re-poll. With about 30 on campus, use six role-assigned groups and paired group debates before individual writing. A technology failure switches to chat plus a downloadable task card; students still submit the same individual output.

These choices draw on [UCL's ABC learning design](https://blogs.ucl.ac.uk/abc-ld/) for sequencing activities around outcomes, the [Community of Inquiry framework](https://coi.athabascau.ca/coi-model/) for online presence, [CAST UDL 3.0](https://udlguidelines.cast.org/) for flexible participation, and [Cornell's active learning guidance](https://teaching.cornell.edu/teaching-resources/active-collaborative-learning/active-learning/getting-started-active-learning) for structured practice and checks for understanding.

## 4. Five-week delivery map

Dates and submission times remain relative. COM7401 completes each week's two core topics on Monday, followed by its Wednesday workshop. COM7411 covers the same two topics on Monday and Saturday. Each topic pack has the same individual output; the live activity differs.


| Week | Shared AM / first topic                                      | Shared PM / second topic                                                    | COM7401 Wednesday                                    | Individual evidence toward portfolio                                             |
| ---- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1    | Induction, research quality and impact case                  | Ontology, epistemology and paradigm choice                                  | Clarify positions; bridge to a researchable problem  | Provisional topic, research-value statement and justified paradigm position      |
| 2    | Funnel, scope and research-question checks                   | Search strategy, critical reading and literature gap                        | Source-search demonstration and ethics preparation   | Revised question, source trail and evaluative literature matrix                  |
| 3    | Quantitative/qualitative/mixed design with ethics checkpoint | Sampling, data source and collection instrument                             | Ethics case application and design troubleshooting   | Method-choice rationale, specific ethics risk/mitigation and collection plan     |
| 4    | Quantitative analysis choice and interpretation              | Qualitative analysis/coding and trustworthiness                             | Tool and referencing demonstration; report checklist | Feasible analysis plan, limitations, full report draft and peer feedback         |
| 5    | Report coherence, reflection and submission checks           | Four-check defence practice, recording support and transfer to project work | Defence clinic and final Q&A                         | Locked report, assigned check, four-minute defence and reflective next-step note |

Prepare a brief method-neutral path for students whose proposals use only one analysis tradition: they practise both at introductory level but develop their chosen one in the portfolio. Provide suitable sample data and a short transcript so exercises never depend on students having collected real research data. The Week 5 report support must occur before the report lock for each track; the assigned check and defence practice occur after the lock. Schedule this relative sequence separately for COM7401 and COM7411 so Saturday teaching is not made redundant by an earlier deadline.

## 5. Development work and quality gates

1. **Confirm schedule and assessment controls.** Verify the start and end times of all five weekly event types and complete the 45-hour ledger. Confirm Moodle's two linked portfolio upload points, report lock, individual check allocation, feedback route and code-specific relative deadlines. Publish a single approved assessment master and one marking rubric; make module-code and calendar information a cover page.
2. **Repair the academic master.** Rebuild the slide edit list from actual decks, correct labels/sequence, condense lecture overload, create the missing task cards and datasets, and map every topic to LO1–LO3 and indicative content. Replace image-only or scanned core information with accessible text. Mark legacy slides/briefs as superseded without deleting source files during development.
3. **Build the two delivery layers.** Write ten campus scripts plus five post-Monday Zoom guides; write ten online scripts with the 1–5, 6–24 and 25–50 activity variants, asynchronous follow-up and technology fallback. Specify the same output and success check for every variant. Give a second tutor enough detail to run each campus session without inventing the teaching method.
4. **Align assessment and moderation.** Define and exemplify the four methodological checks, decide how checks are assigned and released after report lock, reconcile the oral rubric's cap/band contradiction, and run marker calibration on anonymised sample responses. Give students one clear AI-use statement separating report and defence.
5. **Rehearse and publish.** Pilot the ontology debate at three and fifty online, and at about thirty on campus; pilot the literature critique, quantitative choice and qualitative coding tasks with their actual handouts. Check captions, alt text, reading access, links, slide legibility and no staff answer keys in student view. Verify both Moodle shells expose the same master content and each track's correct activities and relative deadlines.

### Acceptance checks

- Every one of the ten core themes has one shared content pack, one COM7401 script and one COM7411 script; all activity variants end in the same assessable individual output.
- The five COM7401 Wednesday guides refer only to COM7401 and follow Monday's teaching. COM7411's equivalent essential learning appears in its Monday/Saturday or guided follow-up route without an extra Zoom obligation.
- Both tracks have a feasible, published path to 45 scheduled hours and 155 independent hours; any timetable shortfall is resolved before release.
- A student and a marker see the same report/defence task and rubric across codes, with clear code-specific deadlines, report lock, challenge release and AI rules.
- The report is due only after both analysis topics and feedback opportunity; the defence is due only after the report is locked, the assigned check is released and a practice opportunity has occurred.
- A tutor unfamiliar with the module can run a campus session from the script; a single online tutor can run each activity at cohorts of three and fifty without changing its learning goal.
