# COM7401 Wednesday Zoom guides — staff only

This is an **additional COM7401 workshop after Monday AM/PM**, including the two COM7401 cohort codes shown together on CELCAT. COM7411 does not attend. The old Zoom guides' “before this morning” wording and “three cohorts combined” assumption are superseded. No essential concept is taught only here; the same core outcomes sit in the shared notes and COM7411 Monday/Saturday sessions. Run each as a 60-minute base until actual event end times are confirmed in the hour ledger. Record or provide an equivalent catch-up prompt. Exit responses are formative and must never become a hidden summative condition.

| Week | 0–10 minutes: retrieval | 10–35: demonstration or case | 35–50: interaction | 50–60: bridge and exit response |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — From philosophy to a question | Ask students which assumption in their Week 1 position most affects evidence. | Show a computing topic narrowed into a problem, question and evidence plan; compare two defensible philosophical routes. | Chat/poll: identify the first vague term in a sample question, then explain why it matters. | Students submit their provisional problem and one term to define before Week 2. Respond to common uncertainties in the next Monday opening. |
| 2 — Search, appraisal and ethics preparation | Retrieve the gap and three-source matrix from Monday. | Demonstrate a search trail and backward/forward citation check; show how to record why a source was included. Give a short primer on consent, risk and data access. | Students submit one candidate source and one critique of its method via chat or form. | Direct all students to complete the Ethics Self-Assessment Worksheet **before Week 3 Monday**, using an asynchronous catch-up route where needed. |
| 3 — Ethics in the actual design | Retrieve Monday's question–method–ethics chain. | Use shared master Topics 05–06 and two computing cases to show how consent, privacy, harm and permissions change a data-collection plan. Keep the legacy ethics deck as staff reference only. | Poll “Which risk is most specific?” then discuss one mitigation; do not imply a poll confers approval. | Each student writes one revised safeguard for their own proposal and identifies where local ethics guidance must be checked. |
| 4 — Analysis and referencing tools | Retrieve a quant or qual analysis choice and its required data. | Demonstrate a small numerical summary and a short coding trail using the shared invented examples. Show a referencing workflow and the Word brief's distinct written/defence GAI rules. | Chat: students identify a claim that the example data cannot support, and a citation that needs checking. | Students complete a report coherence/technology checklist before their report lock. Avoid vendor advertising from the old deck. |
| 5 — Defence clinic | Retrieve the submitted question and explain the difference between a proposal summary and a check response. | Demonstrate a **practice** four-minute methodological defence and the agreed submission route; do not reveal or guess a learner's assigned check. | Anonymous question form or chat Q&A on limits, question wording and recording access. | Students record one concrete improvement to their response and confirm the relative defence milestone. Offer the same information in an accessible catch-up note. |

**Large-group facilitation:** one tutor demonstrates and handles a moderated chat/poll; a second tutor can cluster questions if available. Do not attempt individual draft reviews during a mass call. For each week, the facilitator keeps the three most common misconceptions and sends a short clarification to both COM7401 cohorts. Check the source map before using any legacy slide numbers or book figures.
