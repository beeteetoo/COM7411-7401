# COM7411 Monday/Saturday live online guides — staff only

The ten shared topics run Monday then Saturday each week. COM7411 has **no COM7401 Wednesday Zoom obligation**. The same essential search, ethics, analysis-tool and defence support is embedded here or in accessible follow-up. The module specification describes scheduled live online learning within flexible distributed delivery: post notes, captions/recordings where permitted and an equivalent individual catch-up prompt for learners unable to attend live. Do not make camera use a condition for participation.

**Reusable live cycle:** 0–15 min retrieval/poll; 15–35 short tutor explanation; 35–55 worked computing example; 55–65 screen break; 65–105 active investigation; 105–125 synthesis; 125–135 break; 135–165 individual portfolio studio; 165–180 exit response and feedback. This is a 180-minute core, not a claim about scheduled hours. If a confirmed slot lasts 270 minutes, run a 45-minute second-case challenge, a 10-minute break and 35 minutes of supervised peer review/feedback. Longer and shorter slot allocations must be entered in the hour ledger before publishing. Use one shared document with named roles where group work is used; an accessible downloadable document/chat response is the fallback.

## Week 1 Monday — Foundations and research quality

**Model:** compare a browser-warning build objective with a question about whether it improves phishing recognition. Ask: “What evidence would allow that claim?” Use Student Note 01 and book Chapters 1–3.

- **1–5 learners:** roundtable case dissection; each learner proposes an evidence measure while the tutor tests the claim.
- **6–24:** groups of 3–4 mark problem, evidence, limitation and impact on one shared case board.
- **25–50:** poll on “build versus finding,” then rooms of 4–5 produce one claim/evidence/limitation row; sample three rooms and re-poll.
- **Fallback:** tutor posts the case and response grid in chat; students complete it individually.

**Individual exit output:** 120-word provisional problem and evidence statement. **Check:** it describes an uncertainty, not only a product. Post a summary of common gaps for Saturday.

## Week 1 Saturday — Philosophy and ontology

**Model:** two ways to investigate security-dashboard use; clarify philosophical versus domain ontology. Use Student Note 02 and book Chapter 6, pp. 82–96.

- **1–5:** rotate realist, constructivist and challenger roles around one case. With one learner, the tutor takes the challenge role.
- **6–24:** pairs prepare a claim and counter-position, then regroup to test what evidence each position permits.
- **25–50:** initial poll; rooms of 4–5 with claimant, challenger, evidence checker and reporter complete a shared claim/evidence/counter board; sample reports and re-poll.
- **Fallback:** use two chat columns or a downloadable position card with private written response.

**Individual exit output:** question, chosen position, evidence and credible counter-position. **Check:** no discipline-wide paradigm assertion. Carry the provisional topic into Week 2.

## Week 2 Monday — Funnel and research question

**Model:** field → documented tension → scope → question → evidence, then distinguish an artefact from the finding its evaluation could support. Use Student Note 03 and book Chapter 4.

- **1–5:** live research-question studio, each learner shows one question and receives a tutor/peer probe.
- **6–24:** triads trade six-box funnels and identify undefined terms or inaccessible data.
- **25–50:** poll on two weak questions; breakout pairs use a fixed four-question review card, then post one improved example and reason.
- **Fallback:** use a shared question board with individual written comments.

**Individual exit output:** revised question and two-sentence evidence plan. **Check:** it can be answered by a feasible study. Invite learners to post one real candidate source before Saturday.

## Week 2 Saturday — Search and critical literature

**Model:** live search trail, citation chaining and a critical evidence-matrix row. Use Student Note 04 and book Chapter 5. Do not require PRISMA for every proposal.

- **1–5:** each learner screenshares or describes one candidate paper; group identifies claim, method and limitation.
- **6–24:** groups of 3–4 compare two accessible abstracts, then transfer the structure to their own sources.
- **25–50:** tutor demonstrates search; rooms of 4–5 build a shared comparison row; sample rows and correct source-quality overclaims. Use a form to capture individual progress.
- **Fallback:** provide stable search screenshots/text and downloadable abstracts; accept individual matrix upload.

**Individual exit output:** three-source matrix and 180-word comparative paragraph. **Check:** synthesis ends in a justified gap. Before Week 3, direct learners to the Ethics Self-Assessment Worksheet and a short original ethics primer; this replaces the COM7401 Wednesday preparation.

## Week 3 Monday — Design and ethics

**Model:** question → evidence → design → method → analysis → safeguard, with a design-and-creation and mixed-method example. Use Student Note 05, book Chapter 6 and the separate Ethics Pack. Explain that book ethics-clearance examples are not local approval rules.

- **1–5:** tutor-led methods clinic; each learner defends one design and the tutor proposes an alternative.
- **6–24:** triads review each other's chains and one risk from the completed ethics worksheet.
- **25–50:** poll on suitable design for two scenarios; rooms of 4–5 complete a shared question–method–risk board; sample a few and synthesise.
- **Fallback:** structured design/ethics form with tutor feedback in the discussion board.

**Individual exit output:** design chain, rejected alternative and specific mitigation. **Check:** mixed-method strands have an integration step, and no one claims approval was granted.

## Week 3 Saturday — Sampling and data collection

**Model:** define unit of analysis, sample/source, selection rule and instrument for a simulated alert study. Use Student Note 06 and book Chapter 7, pp. 115–129.

- **1–5:** co-design one instrument item, then each learner applies the five-row table to their own proposal.
- **6–24:** pairs critique a double-barrelled survey item and a weak sample claim before individual planning.
- **25–50:** poll on primary/secondary data and selection bias; rooms of 4–5 fix one instrument, then post the before/after rationale.
- **Fallback:** chat or forum instrument clinic using the same rubric.

**Individual exit output:** sampling/data-source/instrument table and one measure or question. **Check:** no actual recruitment or data collection is required for the portfolio.

## Week 4 Monday — Quantitative analysis

**Model:** use `07 Sample Data.csv` for counts and median response time; distinguish description from causal or population claims. Use Student Note 07 and book Chapter 7, pp. 130–137.

- **1–5:** each learner calculates one measure and critiques a conclusion; tutor checks assumptions.
- **6–24:** pairs compute both interfaces and write warranted/unwarranted interpretations.
- **25–50:** poll on which statistic fits which question; rooms calculate one result in a shared sheet, then tutor checks a sample and shows the worked answer.
- **Fallback:** downloadable CSV/table with calculator-independent instructions and answer reveal after submission.

**Individual exit output:** bounded interpretation of sample data and analysis choice for own proposal. **Check:** method fits data and question. Offer a short optional tool demonstration here because COM7411 has no Wednesday demo.

## Week 4 Saturday — Qualitative analysis

**Model:** read, segment, code and propose a theme from the invented `08 Sample Transcript.md`. Use Student Note 08, book Chapter 6 pp. 109–111 and Chapter 7 pp. 133–135.

- **1–5:** co-code line by line; ask each learner for a rival reading of the same phrase.
- **6–24:** pairs code separately, compare choices, then build a provisional theme.
- **25–50:** individual initial code in a poll/form; rooms of 4–5 negotiate two codes and a theme on a shared board; tutor samples contrasting solutions.
- **Fallback:** downloadable coding table and forum comparison prompt.

**Individual exit output:** coding table, provisional theme, rival reading and method choice. **Check:** invented comments are not empirical findings. Provide referencing/GAI guidance and arrange report peer feedback before its relative lock.

## Week 5 Monday — Report integration and reflection

**Model:** one consistent question across four report headings and one specific methodological trade-off. Use Student Note 09, Chapters 3, 5 and 8 and the approved assessment brief. Run this before the COM7411 report lock.

- **1–5:** roundtable peer review of a short chosen excerpt per learner; tutor asks about question–evidence fit.
- **6–24:** reciprocal pairs use the four-section coherence checklist and give two revisions.
- **25–50:** rooms of 4–5 use assigned reviewer roles; each learner receives feedback on a short excerpt and then completes an individual revision checklist.
- **Fallback:** private peer-review exchange and tutor FAQ on the board.

**Individual exit output:** full report draft, revision list and reflection on a real limitation. **Check:** report does not claim uncollected results. Set a relative report deadline after this opportunity and before assigned check release.

## Week 5 Saturday — Methodological defence and transfer

**Model:** a *practice* check applied to an example proposal. Use Student Note 10 and the approved four-check guide, not the book's full-viva procedure. The student's report must already be locked and assigned check released. If not, practise only with sample checks and adjust the relative deadline.

- **1–5:** each learner gives a short live practice explanation and receives focused tutor feedback; private recording remains an option.
- **6–24:** pairs run timed practice and use a checklist for question, named check, direct application and time.
- **25–50:** rooms of 4–5 rotate candidate, challenger and timekeeper; tutor samples a few issues in plenary rather than hearing every defence.
- **Fallback:** private audio practice/upload and written self-check; no learner must disclose their final response to peers.

**Individual exit output:** a timed practice response and upload readiness check; the final recorded response follows the assigned check and relative deadline. **Check:** the student defends the exact locked question without GAI assistance.

## Online facilitation and access rules

Post task instructions before opening breakouts, appoint roles, give a visible return time and require one shared artifact plus each student's individual output. Invite voice, chat or written input; do not make cameras compulsory. Use captions and accessible notes for mini-inputs. If a learner misses the live slot, provide the same case, a short recorded or written model and the same individual task with tutor/peer response through the board. Use the board to support learning, not to add an unapproved summative requirement.
