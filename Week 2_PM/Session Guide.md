## 1. Core — identical in both threads

### Students leave with
- The ability to distinguish literature-review types (narrative, systematic, scoping, theoretical, meta-analysis) and say when each fits — noting that a review type is *often associated with* a stance but not fixed to it.
- A **critical appraisal of one paper** (their candidate source) through five lenses *and* through their Stance Card.
- A **critical paragraph** (summarise → evaluate → connect) and a note of how a different stance would criticise the paper — the opening of the Critical Literature Review section.

### Pre-session prep (both threads)
- The **Literature Search Task** output: search log, three sources, and a chosen candidate (from Week 2 AM).
- Their **Stance Card** and draft research question.
- Reference material (optional depth, not required for everyone): `Pre-Session Prep/Student_Worksheet_Literature_Reviews.docx`, `Critical Evaluation Checklist.docx`, `Critical Evaluation Worksheet.docx` (the long two-article version — now an optional extension), `Critiquing Research Papers.docx`, `Backward_Forward_Searching_Worksheet.docx`, PRISMA files, `LitReviews.jpg`.

### Core task
`In-Class Activity/Critical Appraisal Worksheet.docx` — Parts 1–6 (snapshot, stance, five lenses, critical paragraph, alternative position, peer swap). `Comparison_of_Literature_Reviews.docx` is kept as a **reference table** of review types, with its stance column corrected to "often associated with (a guide, not a rule)".

### Content delivered live
Purpose of a literature review · the five types with CS/DA/CY examples · choosing a review (and why type does not fix stance) · what critical evaluation is and the key aspects · judging a study by its **own stance's standards** first · the detailed worked example · common pitfalls (one slide).
Cut from the old deck: the whole searching section (now an async task), the advanced-framework slide, roles-of-review infographics, and "doctoral-level" wording.

## 5. Tips and parity check
- Keep the **critical paragraph** — it is raw material for the 900-word Literature Review.
- Watch for students judging a study by the wrong standard (for example criticising an interpretive study for a small sample).
- If a student has no candidate source, supply two or three backup papers from the Week 2 prep folder.
- **Parity check:** both threads produce an appraised source, a critical paragraph and an alternative-stance note; both complete their module's Ethics Self-Assessment before Week 3 Session 1.
