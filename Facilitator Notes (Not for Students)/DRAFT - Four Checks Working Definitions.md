The brief fixes only the names and the marker's test: the recording states the research question, names the assigned check, responds to it directly, and stays within 4 minutes (±30 s). It sets no definitions, so what follows is consistent with the brief but is an interpretation.

Shared frame for all four: the check is a methodological stress test of the student's own research question and design. The student does not recite a definition. They apply the check to their own proposal and say what it shows, including where the proposal is weak and what they would change. Honestly finding a weakness and responding to it scores better than claiming the proposal passes cleanly.

## 1. Build vs Finding

**Working reading:** separates what the study builds in (assumptions, definitions, the chosen framing) from what the study could actually find out. Does the answer come from the data, or was it built into the design?

**Student answers:** If my study returned the result I expect, would that be a finding, or something my design guaranteed? What in my question, sample or instrument is built in rather than discovered?

**Strong:** names one specific built-in assumption (for example a framework that presupposes the effect), says what a genuine finding would look like, and either shows the design can produce surprise or adjusts it.

**Weak:** restates the methodology, or says "my design is neutral" with no example.

**Alternative reading to watch for:** "Build" may mean building an artefact (system, model, prototype) as opposed to producing knowledge. This matters for Computing and Cyber students. If the module lead meant that, the check becomes "is this a design or build project or an empirical one?" Draft prompts below work for both, but confirm if you can.

## 2. Collapse Test

**Working reading:** remove one key element and see whether the proposal still holds. Typical elements are the main concept, the sample, the method, or one of the sub-questions. If the proposal collapses, it is over-dependent on that element; if nothing changes, the element was not doing any work.

**Student answers:** Which single element carries the most weight in my design? If it were removed or failed (access denied, a variable unmeasurable, a method unsuitable), does the study still answer the question? What is the fallback?

**Strong:** picks the real load-bearing element, explains the consequence, and states a justified fallback or an honest limit on the claim.

**Weak:** chooses a trivial element to remove, or says "nothing would collapse" with no reasoning.

## 3. No/Undefined-Word Test

**Working reading:** the research question and aim contain no undefined or vague terms. Words such as "effective", "better", "impact", "awareness", "secure" or "improve" need an operational definition tied to what will be measured or observed. Students who have defined everything should show how.

**Student answers:** Take each key word in my research question. What exactly does it mean in this study, and how will I recognise it in my data?

**Strong:** picks the two or three most contestable terms and defines each in a way that links to the design (measure, indicator, coding category), noting what the definition excludes.

**Weak:** dictionary definitions only, or a definition that cannot be observed or measured.

## 4. "To..." Test

**Working reading:** the proposal's purpose can be stated as a single sentence beginning "To...", with a verb that matches the kind of study (to explore, to compare, to test, to explain, to evaluate). If the sentence cannot be written, or the verb does not fit the methodology, the question and design are misaligned.

**Student answers:** Complete "This study aims to...". Does the verb match what my method can deliver? For example, "to prove" against an interpretivist design, or "to explore" against a hypothesis test.

**Strong:** gives a clean sentence, justifies the verb, and shows the alignment between question, stance and method. Revises the verb if the check exposes a mismatch.

**Weak:** the aim is a topic ("to look at phishing") or a list of tasks, with no link between verb and method.

**Note:** this check is the most closely tied to the Week 1 to 2 work on stance and question types, so it is the easiest one to teach early without giving the allocation away.

## Teaching without pre-empting the allocation

Because the checks are issued in Week 4, teach the underlying skills throughout without naming the four tests:

- Weeks 1 and 2 (stance, question types, funnel): "To..." alignment and undefined terms already fit here.
- Week 3 (design): load-bearing elements, fallbacks, and what the design could and couldn't discover.
- Week 5 AM (defence workshop): all four checks, with a model 4-minute defence of each against a synthetic proposal.

## Recommended allocation mechanism (decision for you)

Use a pre-generated, balanced random list, not a live draw or self-selection.

- Generate once, with a recorded seed, an allocation of every enrolled student ID (both modules) to one of the four checks, as equal as possible in each module.
- Store the list in the module files for moderation. Publish each student's own check on Moodle at the Week 4 issue point (Zoom 4 for COM7401; the Saturday session for COM7411, with the same Moodle release as the fallback).
- A "live reveal" is then just a Moodle release at that moment, so the thread difference disappears and absentees lose nothing.
- Reasons: it is auditable, it is identical across the two modules, it needs no live tooling, and it protects against claims of unfairness.
- Do not allocate by student ID digits or name: students could work it out before Week 4.
- Late enrolments and extensions: allocate the next in rotation and record it.

If you would rather have students pick or swap, say so; I would advise against it, because students will pick the check that looks easiest.

## Marking note

Criterion 4 (20%) rewards stating the question, naming the check, responding directly, and staying in time. It does not reward any particular check result. State this in the moderation notes, so an unlucky allocation is not marked down.
