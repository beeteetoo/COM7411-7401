# Lecturer copy: Thematic Analysis Coding Task (model answer)

**Student sheet:** `Week 4_PM/In-Class Activity/Thematic Analysis Coding Task.docx` (shared by both modules; the data are invented).
**Use:** after the live worked example (smartphone and elderly users). Students should reach something similar to the table below, but there is no single right answer. Reward a clear audit trail from chunk to concept to category.

## One defensible coding

| Participant | Chunk | Concept | Category | Barrier or opportunity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | pops up every time I log in, even on the office network | Frequent interruption | Usability | Barrier |
| A | breaks my concentration | Disrupts work | Usability | Barrier |
| A | I do feel safer since the phishing email | Sense of security | Trust and protection | Opportunity |
| B | I have to use my own phone | Personal device required | Trust and privacy | Barrier |
| B | not comfortable with the company having anything installed | Privacy concern | Trust and privacy | Barrier |
| C | I just approve the notification and carry on | Easy routine use | Usability | Opportunity |
| C | The IT desk helped me set it up in five minutes | Helpful support | Support and understanding | Opportunity |
| D | no signal and can't log in at all | Lock-out when offline | Usability | Barrier |
| D | lost half a day waiting for a code | Lost working time | Usability | Barrier |
| E | I don't really understand what it is protecting | Lack of understanding | Support and understanding | Barrier |
| E | Nobody explained why we need it | No explanation given | Support and understanding | Barrier |
| E | I just click approve without reading | Approving without thought | Security practice | Barrier |
| F | backup codes are in a PDF that I can never find | Hard-to-find recovery codes | Usability | Barrier |
| F | use the same code on the shared accounts | Poor shared-account practice | Security practice | Barrier |

## What the pattern shows
- Usability carries the most barriers (interruption, lock-out when offline, recovery codes).
- Support and understanding has both: help desk support works, but the reason for MFA has not been explained.
- Security practice is a hidden problem: unthinking approval and shared codes weaken the protection MFA is meant to give.
- Possible recommendations: explain the purpose in plain language; provide an offline option; give a secure, findable recovery route; review shared-account practice.

## Points to draw out
- Chunks differ between students; check the concept names the idea, not the wording.
- "Approving without thought" could be a concept or a category. Ask students to justify; there is no single correct answer.
- Six comments are far too few to conclude anything. Press students to say what a bigger dataset would add (frequency, differences between groups).
- Link to rigour: ask how a second coder would test the codes; this feeds the Reflection section.
