# Recording outlines: bridge resources (Weeks 3 to 4)

Recordings are made by the tutor. Outlines only; each is a screen-share with voice-over and captions. They are shared by both modules (upload to both Moodle sites). The decks and primers already exist; these recordings reuse them.

## 1. Sampling and data collection micro-lectures (3 x about 7 minutes)
**Used by:** the online Week 3 AM bridge (Mon to Sat). Campus students use `Week 3_PM/Pre-Session Prep/Sampling and Data Collection Primer.docx` instead.

| Clip | Segments | Source slides / text |
|---|---|---|
| A. Sampling | Population, frame and sample (2 min); probability v non-probability (3 min); size and bias (2 min) | Week 3 PM deck: "Choosing a sample", "Probability and non-probability sampling", "How big, and how biased?"; primer sections 1 to 3 |
| B. Measurement scales | Four scales with a computing example each (4 min); why the scale limits later statistics (3 min) | Deck: "Measurement scales"; primer section 4 |
| C. Instruments | Linking instrument to question (2 min); question types and Likert (2 min); three levels of qualitative question (2 min); piloting (1 min) | Deck: "Designing the instrument", "Qualitative questions work at three levels"; primer section 5 |

## 2. Qualitative worked example (about 15 minutes)
**Used by:** online Week 3 to 4 bridge; campus students see it live in Week 4 PM. Protect it: it is the strongest teaching sequence in the module.
Walk the smartphone and older users example on the Week 4 PM deck, step by step: read and chunk the interview extract, ask "what is the key point?", code the four quotes, move chunks to concepts, group concepts into four categories with Barriers and Opportunities, then draw conclusions (Ease of use has most barriers; documentation and tutorials before technology). Pause on each slide to ask viewers to predict the next step. Finish by pointing to the coding task sheet.

## 3. Tools demo (about 40 minutes; also Zoom 3 live for campus)
Follow the Zoom 3 deck:
- Quantitative: load a small dataset in a spreadsheet and in R or Python or jamovi; describe it; plot it; test one relationship (12 min).
- Qualitative: a coding table in a spreadsheet, and a quick look at NVivo or equivalent if licensed (10 min).
- Referencing: add three sources to Zotero or EndNote, insert citations, build a bibliography, check one wrong entry (8 min).
- Generative AI: the Category B rules from the brief (what is permitted for the report; nothing for the oral defence) and a model declaration paragraph (10 min).
Use invented data and a topic that is unlikely to be a student topic.
