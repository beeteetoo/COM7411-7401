# Model defence: tutor outline (Week 5 AM)

**Purpose:** a tutor-modelled defence on a fictional proposal, delivered live in about four minutes. This is an outline of beats, not a script: deliver it from understanding with prompt words, as students are asked to do. Do not hand the outline to students.

**Status:** written by the course team as a working model. The check definitions are the working definitions in `DRAFT - Four Checks Working Definitions.md`; update this file if the module lead supplies different ones.

## The fictional proposal (also on the deck)
- Question: does a short phishing-awareness programme reduce click rates in a small firm?
- Design: mixed methods. Click rates before and after (quantitative) plus staff interviews (qualitative).
- Check given: the Collapse Test.

## Beats for the Collapse Test (about 4 minutes)
| Time | Beat | What to say (in your own words) |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Question | State the research question exactly as it appears in the (fictional) report |
| 0:30 | Check | Name the Collapse Test and say what it asks: remove the element carrying most weight, does the study still answer the question? |
| 1:00 | Load-bearing element | The before-and-after click-rate comparison carries most weight. If the firm will not release click data, the quantitative arm collapses |
| 1:45 | Does the study survive? | The interviews alone could still answer a different question (why people click) but not the stated one (does training reduce clicking). Say so honestly |
| 2:30 | Fallback | Simulated phishing emails sent by the researcher, with the firm's consent, would generate the click data; or restate the question as exploratory |
| 3:15 | Limit the claim | Without firm data the study cannot claim a reduction, only a pattern of reported awareness |
| 3:45 | Close | One sentence: question and method are aligned because the click data answer "how much" and the interviews answer "why", and the fallback keeps both |

## Short beats for the other three checks (use as a second demonstration if time allows)
- **Build vs Finding:** the training content itself tells staff what to look for, so clicking less could be built in by teaching to the test. Finding would require a later simulated phish that differs from the training examples.
- **No/Undefined-Word Test:** "awareness programme" and "reduce" are undefined. Define "reduce" as a drop in click rate on a stated set of simulated emails over a stated period; define the programme by its length and content.
- **"To..." Test:** "To evaluate whether a short training programme reduces click rates on simulated phishing emails." The verb "evaluate" fits a before-and-after design; "to prove" would not.

## Debrief questions for students
1. Which element did the defender say carries the most weight, and was it the right one?
2. Did the defender find a real weakness, or just claim the proposal passes?
3. What did they say they would change, or limit?
4. Was it within four minutes, and did it sound like thinking aloud?
