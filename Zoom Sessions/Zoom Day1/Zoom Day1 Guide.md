## Calibration extracts (invented for teaching — not real papers)

**Extract A.** *"We hypothesised that adaptive thresholding would reduce false positives compared with fixed thresholding. Eighty network traces were randomly assigned to one of two conditions, and detection rates were compared using a two-tailed t-test (α = 0.05). Each run was repeated 30 times to assess reliability. We expect the findings to generalise to similar enterprise networks."*
→ **Positivist.** Clues: hypothesis, random assignment, statistical test, repetition for reliability, generalisation.

**Extract B.** *"We conducted semi-structured interviews with 14 security operations analysts to understand how they experience alert fatigue. Participants were purposively sampled. Interviews were audio-recorded, transcribed and coded inductively, and themes were developed iteratively. The first author, a former analyst, kept a reflexive journal to examine how their own experience shaped the interpretation. We do not claim statistical generalisation; readers should judge transferability to their own settings."*
→ **Interpretivist.** Clues: "experience", purposive sample, inductive coding, reflexivity, transferability instead of generalisation.

**Extract C.** *"Starting from a practical problem reported by a regional hospital — clinicians were ignoring security prompts — we designed and built a prototype prompt interface. We evaluated it with a usability test (n = 24, task completion times) followed by focus groups to understand why some prompts were still ignored. We combined the quantitative and qualitative data because each answered a different part of the problem; the design was judged by whether it worked on the ward."*
→ **Pragmatist.** Clues: problem-led, built an artefact, mixed methods justified by the problem, judged by usefulness.

**Extract D (the trap).** *"We surveyed 212 employees about their attitudes to password policies. Respondents frequently described the policies as 'pointless rituals'. We interpret these accounts as evidence that security norms are socially negotiated in each team rather than fixed rules."*
→ **Leans interpretivist despite using a survey.** Clues: respondents' own descriptions, "interpret", "socially negotiated". Teaching point: **a survey is a method, not a stance** — look at what the authors claim and how they treat meaning.

Accept "mixed" or "unclear" answers where the student explains why; the learning is in the clues, not the label.
